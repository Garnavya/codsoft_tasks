Lesson 01

What is Phishing? Attack Vectors & Taxonomy

Phishing is a form of social engineering where attackers deceive individuals into surrendering confidential information (such as login credentials, financial identifiers, or personal data) or deploying malicious software.

1. Email Phishing (Mass Bulk Phishing)

The most traditional and ubiquitous attack vector. Threat actors send millions of generic emails posing as familiar institutions (banks, shipping carriers, cloud providers). Attackers rely on volume: even if only 0.1% of recipients respond, the campaign is profitable.

Example: "Your cloud storage is full! Upgrade your quota by logging in here."

2. Spear Phishing (Targeted Reconnaissance)

Unlike bulk phishing, spear phishing targets a specific organization or individual. Attackers conduct extensive reconnaissance on LinkedIn, corporate websites, and social media to craft tailored messages referencing real colleagues, ongoing projects, or specific vendors.

Example: "Hi Priya, here is the updated Q3 budget spreadsheet we discussed in yesterday's engineering sync."

3. Whaling (Executive Impersonation)

A high-stakes subset of spear phishing directed against executive leadership—CEOs, CFOs, Board Members, or Treasury managers. The goal is typically to coerce multi-million dollar wire transfers (Business Email Compromise) or obtain proprietary intellectual property.

4. Smishing (SMS Phishing) & Vishing (Voice Phishing)

Smishing: Phishing conducted via SMS text messages, frequently impersonating package tracking alerts, bank KYC verification, or urgent utility shutoffs.
Vishing: Voice-based manipulation over phone calls. Attackers spoof official caller IDs to pose as law enforcement, fraud departments, or enterprise IT helpdesk technicians.

5. Quishing (QR Code Phishing)

Threat actors embed malicious phishing URLs inside QR codes on physical posters, parking meters, restaurant tables, or even within email PDFs. Because traditional security gateways cannot inspect pixelated images as easily as plain text links, quishing directs victims onto mobile browsers outside enterprise monitoring.

6. Clone Phishing

The attacker intercepts or obtains a genuine, previously delivered email containing attachments or links. They create an exact replica (clone) of the email, replace the authentic links or attachments with weaponized copies, and send it from a spoofed address claiming: "Resending with updated attachment."
