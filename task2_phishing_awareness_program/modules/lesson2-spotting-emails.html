6 Essential Email Red Flags Cheat Sheet

1. Sender Domain Discrepancy

Display names can be set to anything. Always check the actual domain after the '@' symbol. Look for subtle character swaps (like 'paypa1.com' or 'rn' mimicking 'm').

2. Artificial Urgency

Threats of immediate account closure, legal action, or missed delivery within 24 hours are designed to hijack emotional centers and circumvent logical verification.

3. Generic Salutations

Legitimate organizations with whom you hold an account address you by your registered name, not impersonal greetings like 'Dear Customer' or 'Dear Member'.

4. Mismatched Hyperlink Destinations

Anchor text saying 'Click here to login' often points to an entirely different malicious IP or domain. Always hover your cursor or inspect raw links before clicking.

5. Dangerous File Attachments

Watch out for double extensions (e.g. document.pdf.exe), macro-enabled spreadsheets (.xlsm), or password-protected archives designed to bypass antivirus scanners.

6. Unusual Language or Style

Awkward phrasing, uncharacteristic formality, spelling errors, or unnatural requests from colleagues indicate potential account compromise or machine translation.
